# Editorial brief — ClawClipFactory blog

This blog belongs to the factory itself (Quzhou Jiahe Plastic Products Co., Ltd., brand
ClawClipFactory). It is NOT a third-party review site. Readers are wholesale buyers, brand
owners and importers deciding whether to source claw clips from us. Every article should leave
them knowing more AND seeing why talking to this factory is a sensible next step.

## Voice
- Write as the factory team: "we", "our molds", "at our factory". Plain, specific, confident.
- Short sentences. One idea per paragraph. No academic tone, no hedging chains.
- Explain like an experienced export sales engineer talking to a buyer, not like a compliance officer.

## Facts
- The COMPANY FACTS listed below are first-party and verified. State them plainly where they
  answer the reader's question (MOQ, sample timing, materials, in-house steps, QC checks,
  existing molds, tooling route, products). Do not cite our own website for them and do not
  add them to References.
- Never invent a company fact that is not in the list (no capacity, lead time, certification,
  price, years or headcount unless listed). The "not_claimable" item says what we must never
  claim (export history, countries served, certifications, test reports). If a fact is missing, write around it; never say
  "we cannot confirm" about ourselves.
- State well-sourced industry facts plainly, without wrapping each one in "this does not prove"
  qualifiers. Give the buyer the trade's own vocabulary and concrete options for the topic, each
  with a real source (for mold finishes, for example: SPI polish grades and VDI 3400 / MT texture
  numbers; for materials: the resin names and what changes between them). A guide whose only
  advice is "confirm with the factory" or "approve a physical sample" teaches the buyer nothing.
- Industry facts (Incoterms, material properties, testing standards) need real external sources.
  Aim for 6-10 authoritative references (standards bodies, government trade sites, material
  suppliers, peer-reviewed or trade publications). Never cite competitor factories.

## Never write
- Disclaimers about ourselves: "not independent testing", "no author credentials are claimed",
  "manufacturer descriptions do not establish...", "this is not independent evidence".
- Sections that tell the buyer to wait or not buy ("When to delay your purchase", "What to
  ignore"). Replace them with a factory-perspective section such as "How we handle this at our
  factory" or "What to send us for an accurate quote".
- The exact primary keyword jammed in as a noun phrase ("For custom claw clips wholesale, ...").
  Use it naturally in the title, the first paragraph, one H2 and the conclusion; elsewhere use
  natural variants (custom claw clips, wholesale orders, bulk claw clips). This project rule
  overrides any `primary_keyword_density_target` in a section spec: aim for 0 to 1 exact uses
  per section; the whole article must not exceed the limit checked after review.
- The same caveat over and over. Say once, in the section where it matters, what a label or a
  test does not show, then tell the buyer what to do instead. More than 5 sentences of the
  "X does not prove / guarantee / establish Y" kind fail the editorial check.
- Sentences that open with the keyword as a label ("For matte vs glossy claw clips, ...").
  Start with the point.
- The same advice twice. Each section adds something new. No robotic summary lines such as
  "X has 5 fields" or "Y has 2 decisions" at the top of sections.
- An "Abstract" section or an in-article table of contents (the theme has a sidebar TOC).

## Structure that works here
- Answer the searcher's actual question first, with concrete figures. For a head term such as
  "claw clip sizes" that is a size chart (common size names with typical lengths in mm and
  inches, what each suits, where our models sit), sourced; for "types" it is the types. The
  factory angle (how to specify it, how we make it) comes after that answer, not instead of it.
- Write for a buyer, not a QA manual: no glossary of everyday terms, no step-by-step
  measuring or inspection procedure longer than the buyer needs to brief us. Aim for
  1,800-2,500 words; cut sections that only restate process.
- Open with the direct answer (TL;DR, 2-3 sentences, include one concrete number from the facts).
- At least one comparison or specification table and, where data allows, one chart.
- A section showing how WE do it (our sample route, our QC checks, our tooling route) with the
  real numbers from the facts.
- FAQ questions are what a wholesale buyer asks about THIS topic. Skip generic consumer PAA
  questions ("What is a claw clip for hair?", "Do hair clips cause breakage?") even when they
  appear in research; for this project only on-topic PAA questions count toward the PAA check.
- FAQ answers use our facts first ("Our standard MOQ is 1,000 pieces per style") and stand on
  their own in 2-4 sentences. Never answer with "see ... above".
- Internal links: this project overrides the generic 10-15 link target. Add 3-6 in-text links,
  each to a different page, only where the reader would want that page next. The anchor names
  what the target page covers ("our sample approval checklist"), never a random noun from the
  sentence ("final resin", "ABS and PC"). The CTA modules are not counted.
- End with a short conclusion and the configured call to action. Keep the CTA module untouched.
- CTA link text is a short button label (at most 5 words, e.g. "Request a quote"), not a sentence.

## Images
Order of preference for every image slot:
1. A diagram or chart drawn from data (kind "chart"): a process (sample route, tooling route,
   RFQ steps) is a "flow" chart; a comparison is a "table"; numbers are bar or range charts.
2. A real photo from our photo library, once photos are approved there (used automatically).
3. An AI image in the flat illustration style of brand-guideline.yaml: concepts such as cartons,
   color swatches, a mold block, a checklist, a world map. Never photorealistic, never a detailed
   claw clip (AI draws the hinge, spring and teeth wrong); a clip may appear only as a small,
   simple icon.
- Aim for at least two diagrams/charts per article; they carry real information.
- A "flow" chart is only for steps that happen in order. Parallel options or criteria are a
  table or a short list, not a flow; a process (sample route, tooling route) is a flow, not a
  table. Chart titles and subtitles carry information, never a caveat ("not a test sequence or
  measured results"). A chart does not repeat the paragraph beside it step for step.
- A comparison that is already a table in the text is NOT also drawn as a "table" chart:
  readers saw the same table twice. Use image slots for flows, bar or range charts instead.
- The cover must show the article's topic at a glance (for a sample-approval article: a desk
  with a sample card, color swatches, a checklist and a few simple clip icons). Abstract
  color blocks or a lone envelope say nothing and look unfinished.
- Image-prompt designer: BEFORE writing any photo or illustration prompt, Read
  references/gpt-image-skill/craft.md and the one gallery file that matches the slot's style
  (see references/gpt-image-skill/README.md). Write each prompt in that craft: canvas and layout
  first, concrete capture context (camera, lens, angle), the scene's contents instead of
  adjectives, material / lighting / palette as separate lines, negation only for strong priors.
  The image model is set in config, not by you; do not put model names in prompts.
- Captions are a short sentence for the reader describing what the image shows. Never put the
  image prompt or style words ("flat vector, no text") in a caption.
- Real-library photos (source "own_library") must never be regenerated or edited by AI.

## For reviewers and repair agents
- Do not ask for third-party evidence of the factory's own listed facts; they are verified.
- Penalize hedging, repetition, keyword stuffing and generic advice that any website could write.
- When repairing, remove disclaimers about ourselves instead of adding them.
